Guide to eye comfort

Rest your eyes: less eye strain from screens.

To rest your eyes during screen time, follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 seconds. Match screen brightness to the room, make text large enough to read comfortably, blink often and keep the screen about an arm's length away. In the evening, a warmer, dimmer screen is gentler on tired eyes in a dim room. Eye doctors say screen blue light isn't known to damage your eyes: strain comes mostly from long focusing, less blinking and glare.

Why screens tire your eyes

Looking at a screen up close keeps your eye muscles focused for a long time, and people blink much less while they read a screen, so eyes get dry. Glare, a screen much brighter or darker than the room, and small text all add to the strain. The result, often called digital eye strain, shows up as tired, dry or burning eyes, blurry vision and headaches. It usually goes away with rest.

Give your eyes a rest

  1. 20-20-20Every 20 minutes, look about 20 feet (6 meters) away for 20 seconds. It lets your focusing muscles relax.
  2. BlinkBlink fully and often. If your eyes feel dry, artificial tears can help.
  3. Right brightnessThe screen shouldn't glow brighter than the room. In the evening, turn it down, and warm its color.
  4. Distance and sizeKeep the screen about an arm's length away, a little below eye level, and make text big enough to read without leaning in.

Questions

Does blue light from screens damage your eyes?

Eye doctors say there's no evidence that the blue light from screens damages your eyes. Screen eye strain comes from long focusing, less blinking and glare. Blue light does matter for sleep, which is why warmer screens make sense in the evening.

Does a blue light filter reduce eye strain?

Many people find a warm, dim screen more comfortable in the evening, but breaks, blinking, brightness and text size make the biggest difference for eye strain. Use the filter for comfort and sleep, and the 20-20-20 rule for your eyes.

Is dark mode better for your eyes?

It depends on the room. Dark mode is comfortable in a dim room, while light mode is often easier to read in bright light. Dozelight's Darkroom filter goes further at night, with black pages and red text.

When should I see an eye doctor?

If eye strain, blurry vision or headaches keep coming back despite breaks, get your eyes checked. You may need glasses for screen distance, or a different prescription.
