f.lux alternatives for Mac and Windows.

If a gentle evening tint is all you want, you may not need another app: Night Shift on Mac and Night Light on Windows are free and built in. On Windows, LightBulb is a free, open-source app with more control. Iris and CareUEyes are paid apps for Mac and Windows that add dimming, presets and break reminders. Dozelight, our own app, goes down to 1000K and pure red and follows your bedtime, for $9.99 once. And f.lux itself is still free for personal use.

The options, one by one

f.lux Mac, Windows, Linux · free

f.lux is still around and still free: on Mac and Linux, and on Windows for personal use (businesses on Windows buy a license). It goes down to about 1200K, and its Darkroom mode removes blue and green completely while inverting colors. If it works for you, there's no need to switch. People who look elsewhere usually want something already built in, red light without inverted colors, a schedule built around bedtime rather than wake-up time, or extras like dimming and break reminders.

Night Shift Mac · free, built in

Find it in System Settings → Displays → Night Shift. It runs from sunset to sunrise or on your own hours, and its warmest setting is about 2700K, a warm orange. For many evenings that's enough. See how to make Night Shift warmer for its limits and ways past them.

Night Light Windows · free, built in

Find it in Settings → System → Display → Night light. Windows 11 has a Strength slider, and you can schedule it from sunset to sunrise or set hours. Like Night Shift, it stops at a warm orange. See how to make your screen red at night on Windows for going further.

LightBulb Windows · free, open source

A small open-source app (MIT license) that moves the screen from cool daylight to a warm yellow at night. It follows sunrise and sunset for your location or times you set, changes smoothly, lets you keep color-sensitive apps untinted and works without an internet connection. Its night slider goes down to 2500K by default. It's actively maintained, with its latest release in September 2026.

Iris Windows, Mac, Linux · paid, 7-day free trial

Iris has nine preset types, such as Health, Sleep, Reading and Programming, in automatic or manual mode, and adjusts brightness in a way its maker says avoids PWM flicker. You can buy a one-time license for one version or a subscription. Its free sibling, Iris mini, has no settings to speak of: at night it moves to 3400K and 80% brightness automatically.

CareUEyes Windows and Mac · paid, 7-day free trial

CareUEyes combines a blue light filter with eight preset modes, a dimmer that goes down to 1% brightness, and break reminders, including the 20-20-20 rule. Its filter can follow sunrise and sunset. It costs $2.90 a month, $19.90 a year, or $24.90 once for one PC ($39.90 for three).

Dozelight Mac and Windows · our app, $9.99 once

Dozelight is built for late nights: it goes down to 1000K, then to Red Only, which removes blue and green completely, with adjustable brightness. Auto mode warms the screen gradually through the evening, switches to red after your bedtime and fades back before you wake. It adds Black & White, Sepia, Darkroom and Extra Dim filters. On Mac it works together with Night Shift and tints the Touch Bar. $9.99 once during early access (later $14.99), with a 14-day free trial. One license covers up to 3 computers, Mac or Windows. Which one should you pick?

  • A gentle evening tint is enough: use Night Shift on Mac or Night Light on Windows. They're free and already there.
  • You want free and much warmer: f.lux, on Mac or Windows.
  • You want free and open source on Windows: LightBulb.
  • You want dimming, presets and break reminders in one app: Iris or CareUEyes. Both have a 7-day free trial, so try them before you buy.
  • You want the warmest light, pure red at night and a schedule around your bedtime: Dozelight for Mac or Dozelight for Windows.
  • You're on Linux: f.lux has a Linux version, and Gammastep is a free, open-source option.

Questions

Is f.lux still free?

Yes. f.lux is free on Mac and Linux, and free for personal use on Windows. Businesses using it on Windows need a license from its makers.

What is the best free alternative to f.lux?

Start with what's built in: Night Shift on Mac and Night Light on Windows. They don't go as warm as f.lux, but for a mild evening tint they're enough. On Windows, LightBulb is a free, open-source app with more settings.

Which alternative can make the screen pure red?

Dozelight's Red Only removes blue and green completely, with adjustable brightness. f.lux's Darkroom also keeps only red but inverts colors, so pages turn black with red text. Night Shift and Night Light stop at a warm orange.

Can I run two of these apps at once?

It's best not to. Apps that change screen colors can override each other, so pick one and turn the rest off, including Night Light on Windows. The exception is Dozelight on Mac, which is made to work on top of Night Shift.
