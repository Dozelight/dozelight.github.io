Guide for Windows

How to make your screen red at night on Windows.

Windows has no built-in red screen. Night Light (Settings → System → Display → Night light) only goes to a warm orange, even at full strength, and the Color filters in Accessibility offer grayscale and inverted colors but no red. For pure red, with no blue or green at all, use an app like Dozelight and turn on Red Only in its panel.

Getting a red screen

At full strength Night Light lowers blue light but keeps plenty of green, so white turns orange rather than red. The built-in Color filters (Settings → Accessibility → Color filters) are made for color blindness and reading, with grayscale and inverted options, but no red tint.

Questions

Why can't Night Light turn the screen red?

Night Light lowers blue light only, and keeps green. Red needs both blue and green removed, which Night Light doesn't do.

Is red light better at night?

Research suggests that the blue part of light has the strongest effect on melatonin, the hormone that helps you fall asleep, and red light the weakest. Dozelight and Night Light are comfort tools, not medical products.

Why doesn't the screen change color with HDR on?

With HDR turned on, Windows ignores color changes from apps. Turn HDR off in Settings → System → Display while you use a red screen.

Does Dozelight need administrator rights?

No. Windows limits how warm apps can make the screen, and Dozelight can lift that limit once, with an administrator's approval. Everything else works without it.
