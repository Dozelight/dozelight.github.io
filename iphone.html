Dozelight for Mac

Free guide for iPhone and iPad

A red screen at night on iPhone and iPad.

Dozelight is a Mac app, and iPhone and iPad don't allow apps to tint the whole screen. But they have settings built in that get you a red, extra-dim screen at night. They're just well hidden. Here's how to set them up, switch them with a triple-click, and have them turn on by themselves at bedtime.

Works on iPhone and iPad with iOS or iPadOS 16 or later. The pictures are illustrations, so your screen may look slightly different.

  1. 1

    Turn the screen red

    Open Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters. Turn Color Filters on and choose Color Tint.

    Drag Intensity all the way up, then move Hue until the screen is red (both ends of the slider are red).

    This is the iPhone version of Dozelight's Red Only mode: no blue or green light at all.

  2. 2

    Make it extra dim

    Go back to Display & Text Size, turn on Reduce White Point and drag its slider up. At 80 to 100% the screen gets far darker than the lowest brightness setting.

    This matches Dozelight's Extra Dim. Pick a level where you can still read comfortably in the dark.

  3. 3

    Switch it with a triple-click

    Open Settings → Accessibility → Accessibility Shortcut (at the bottom) and tick Color Filters and Reduce White Point.

    Now triple-click the side button to switch red mode on or off. On an iPad, use the top button. On an iPhone or iPad with a Home button, triple-click Home.

    With both ticked, a triple-click shows a small menu so you can choose which one to switch.

  4. 4

    Make it automatic at bedtime

    Open the Shortcuts app, go to Automation and tap +. Choose Time of Day (for example 22:30, daily), or Sleep to follow your sleep schedule's wind-down. Choose Run Immediately, then New Blank Automation.

    Add the actions Set Color Filters and Set White Point, both set to On. Make a second automation for the morning with both set to Off.

    Search for “Color Filters” and “White Point” in the actions list to find them quickly.

More ways to go easy on your eyes

A warmer Night Shift

Open Settings → Display & Brightness → Night Shift, turn on Scheduled and drag Color Temperature to More Warm. It's gentler than full red for the early evening.

Darkroom: black pages with red text

Turn on Classic Invert in Display & Text Size together with the red Color Tint from step 1. White pages turn black and text turns red, the gentlest way to read in bed. You can add Classic Invert to the Accessibility Shortcut too.

Grayscale

In Color Filters, choose Grayscale instead of Color Tint. It makes the phone less tempting to keep scrolling late at night.

On a Mac? Dozelight does all of this for you.

Warmer than Night Shift, down to candlelight and pure red, with a bedtime schedule that changes gradually through the evening. Plus Darkroom, Extra Dim, and the Touch Bar, pointer and keyboard.
