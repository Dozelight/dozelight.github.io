Guide to blue light, for Mac and Windows

Blue light filter, glasses or screen protector: what works.

A blue light filter makes the screen warmer by turning its blue light down. For the evening, the options that make the clearest difference are free: a software filter (Night Shift on a Mac, Night Light on Windows), a dimmer screen and putting it away earlier. Clear “blue light” glasses and screen protectors cut only part of the blue, and a Cochrane review found that the glasses may not ease eye strain. Amber or red glasses block far more, with mixed results for sleep. To remove the screen's blue light completely, down to pure red, use an app that goes further than the built-in filters, such as f.lux or our app, Dozelight.

What a blue light filter does

Screens make white by mixing red, green and blue light. A blue light filter turns the blue part down, and warmer settings turn green down too, so white looks like a light bulb, then a candle, then an ember. The filter works on the whole screen, and screenshots keep their true colors.

Filters are usually used in the evening. Light in the hours before bed can hold back melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy, and push your body clock later (Gooley et al., 2011; Chang et al., 2015), and short-wavelength blue light has the strongest effect on melatonin (Brainard et al., 2001). See screens, blue light and sleep for what else helps.

Software filters Night Shift, Night Light, f.lux, Dozelight

A software filter changes the colors the screen sends out, so it costs little or nothing, needs nothing on your face and can follow a schedule. How far it goes depends on the app: Night Shift and Night Light stop at a warm orange that still lets some blue through, while f.lux and our app, Dozelight, go much warmer (see the table below, the full comparison and f.lux alternatives).

Color is only half of it. In a small lab study with an iPad at full brightness, Night Shift held melatonin back about as much at its warmest setting as at its coolest (Nagare et al., 2019). Changing the color without lowering the brightness may not be enough.

Screen protectors stick-on and clip-on filters

Blue light screen protectors are clear or slightly yellow sheets that sit over the display. In one measurement, a glass blue-light protector cut blue light at 450 nm by about a third to almost a half on iPhones and an iPad, roughly half as much as Night Shift at full strength, and both Night Shift and lower brightness cut it more (Smith et al., 2020). A protector keeps colors close to normal, but it works all day, can't get warmer as the evening goes on and covers only the device it's on.

Blue light glasses clear lenses vs amber and red lenses

Clear or lightly tinted “computer” glasses filter a small part of the blue. A Cochrane review of 17 trials found that they may not reduce eye strain from computer use, that the results for sleep were mixed, and that none of the trials had looked at the health of the macula, the center of the retina (Singh et al., 2023). The American Academy of Ophthalmology doesn't recommend blue light glasses for computer use.

Amber, orange or red lenses are different: they block much more blue light, from lamps and the TV as well as the screen. Trials of wearing them in the hours before bed show mixed but sometimes positive results for sleep, and the benefit seems larger for people with insomnia and some other conditions than for good sleepers (Shechter et al., 2020). They turn everything orange, so they suit winding down more than working.

Dimmer, and off earlier free, and it works on any device

Brightness matters too. Bright light affects melatonin more than dim light (Gooley et al., 2011), and in the measurement above, halving the brightness cut blue light by about 70% (Smith et al., 2020). The simplest step of all is to stop earlier: the American Academy of Sleep Medicine suggests turning off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bed, and the American Academy of Ophthalmology suggests limiting screen time in the two to three hours before bed and using your devices' night settings.

Which one should you use?

  • A gentler evening screen. Turn on Night Shift or Night Light with a schedule and lower the brightness. It's free and already there.
  • Warmer, or pure red late at night. Use a stronger app: f.lux is free, and our app, Dozelight, goes to 1000K and pure red and follows your bedtime.
  • Tired, dry or strained eyes. Blue light glasses may not help. Take breaks, blink and follow the 20-20-20 rule; see rest your eyes.
  • Lamps and the TV too. Amber or red glasses filter the blue from all the light around you, not just the screen. Dimmer, warmer room lights help as well.

Questions

What's the difference between a blue light filter and a blue light blocker?

A filter reduces blue light, like Night Shift and Night Light do. A blocker removes it completely. On a screen that means switching the blue light off, which Dozelight does at 1900K and warmer and in Red Only. Blue light blocking glasses do the same for everything you see, with amber or red lenses.

Do blue light filters help you sleep?

They cut the part of screen light that affects melatonin the most (Brainard et al., 2001), and the American Academy of Ophthalmology suggests trying night settings in the evening. But color alone may not be enough: in one small study at full brightness, Night Shift held melatonin back about as much at its warmest as at its coolest (Nagare et al., 2019). A warm, dim screen works best as part of good sleep habits, not on its own.

Is a blue light filter bad for your eyes?

No. A filter only changes the screen's colors. Colors look warmer while it's on, so turn it off for color-critical work like photo editing.

Does blue light from screens damage your eyes?

The American Academy of Ophthalmology says there's no scientific evidence that blue light from digital devices damages the eye. It links tired, dry eyes after screen work to something simpler: most of us blink less while looking at a screen.

Are blue light glasses better than a screen filter?

For the screen alone, usually not. Clear blue light glasses filter only a little blue and may not help with eye strain (Singh et al., 2023), while a screen filter is free or cheap and can change gradually through the evening. Amber or red glasses make sense if you also want to filter room lights before bed.

Are blue light screen protectors worth it?

Only if you want a protector anyway. In one measurement, a blue-light protector cut less blue light than Night Shift at full strength or lower brightness (Smith et al., 2020), and it can't change through the evening.
