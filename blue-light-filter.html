Guide for Mac and Windows

Block blue light on Mac and Windows.

A blue light filter makes the screen warmer by turning down its blue light, usually in the evening. Macs have one built in, Night Shift (System Settings → Displays → Night Shift), and so does Windows, Night Light (Settings → System → Display → Night light). Both stop at a warm orange that still lets some blue through: they reduce blue light but don't block it. To block blue light completely, down to pure red, use a blue light blocker app like Dozelight, which also changes the filter gradually around your bedtime.

What a blue light filter does

Screens make white by mixing red, green and blue light. A blue light filter turns the blue part down, and warmer settings turn green down too, so white looks like a light bulb, then a candle, then an ember. The filter works on the whole screen, and screenshots keep their true colors.

Filters are usually used in the evening, because light in the hours before bed can delay melatonin, the hormone that makes you sleepy, and blue light has the strongest effect. See screens, blue light and sleep for what research says and what else helps.

Set up a blue light filter

  1. On a MacOpen System Settings → Displays → Night Shift…, drag Color Temperature to More Warm and choose a schedule. More detail.
  2. On WindowsOpen Settings → System → Display → Night light, drag Strength to the right and turn on the schedule. More detail.
  3. To filter all blueInstall Dozelight for Mac or for Windows and turn on Red Only, or Auto to follow your bedtime.

Questions

What's the difference between a blue light filter and a blue light blocker?

A filter reduces blue light, like Night Shift and Night Light do. A blocker removes it completely. On a screen that means switching the blue light off, which Dozelight does at 1900K and warmer and in Red Only. Blue light blocking glasses do the same for everything you see, with amber or red lenses.

Do blue light filters help you sleep?

They cut the part of screen light that affects melatonin the most, which is why sleep experts often suggest them for evening screen use. Studies on filters alone show modest effects, because brightness, how late you're up and what you're doing matter too. A warm, dim screen works best as part of good sleep habits, not on its own.

Is a blue light filter bad for your eyes?

No. A filter only changes the screen's colors. Colors look warmer while it's on, so turn it off for color-critical work like photo editing.

Are blue light glasses better than a screen filter?

Glasses filter all the light you see, including room lights, while a screen filter only changes the screen. A screen filter is free or cheap, needs nothing on your face, and can change gradually through the evening.
